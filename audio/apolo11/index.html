Apolo11

Eight voices, one supply rail and a small computer that keeps them in tune. Mostly.

Apolo11 is what happens when an organism is taught to play chords. It descends from Apolo9: the same logic chips abused as oscillators, the same echo chips, the same habit of every part listening to every other part through the power supply. But each of its eight voice cards now follows the keyboard, through an exponential converter, a filter of CMOS inverters and an envelope of its own.

A tiny tuning computer measures every card and corrects it. A knob called TAME decides how much anyone listens to it. All the way up, it is a polite polysynth within a cent or two. All the way down, the cards drift with the warmth of the room, pull on their neighbours and murmur when they should be quiet. Forty-eight presets in eight drawers, from dub chords to acid bass to the organism itself.

“Eight voices. One rail. Some disagreement.”

  • _ 8 voice cards · self-tuning · TAME from polite to feral
  • _ 2 PT2399s simulated bit by bit · 48 presets in 8 drawers
