Synthesizers

01.Tyrell80

The Yamaha CS-80, modelled chip by chip from the service manual.

In 1977 Yamaha built a synthesizer that weighed as much as a small piano and sounded like the sky opening. Studios that owned one kept it forever. Studios that didn't just kept hoping.

Tyrell80 is that instrument, rebuilt from its circuits. It was modelled chip by chip from the original service manual, with each stage of the voice following the engineers' own drawings.

The brass swells as it should, the aftertouch breathes, and the strings hang in the air a little longer than you expect. The model and the schematics notes are open on GitHub.

“It took four men to carry it upstairs. Now it takes one click.”

  • _ Yamaha CS-80, 1977
  • _ Polyphonic synthesizer
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/tyrell80

02.OBXTRA

Three Oberheim voice boards, modelled from the service manuals.

The OB-X was the loud one. The OB-Xa grew up a little smoother. The OB-8 learned some manners and kept its muscles. Together they defined the sound of a decade of arenas.

OBXTRA models the voice boards of all three, each from its own service manual. They run on OB-Xf's proven open-source engine, presets included.

Choose a brother and the character changes, as it did on the real machines. All three are wide, warm and impossible to bury in a mix.

“Three brothers. Same family. Very different tempers.”

  • _ Oberheim OB-X · OB-Xa · OB-8, 1979
  • _ Polyphonic synthesizer
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/obxtra

03.DXDX7

Dexed's FM engine plus a model of the DX7 hardware.

Six operators and thirty-two algorithms gave 1983 its electric pianos, its glassy bells and its slap bass. They also gave a generation of musicians a manual nobody finished reading.

DXDX7 runs Dexed's faithful open-source FM engine and adds a model of the DX7 hardware: the converters and the output stage that put the grit into the shimmer.

Load your old cartridges and you'll know the sound at once. You'll also find out what it was missing all these years.

“You've heard this bell on a thousand records.”

  • _ Yamaha DX7, 1983
  • _ FM synthesizer
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/dxdx7

04.Youth106

The Juno-106: one oscillator, one filter and that chorus.

Some instruments age. This one just collects more records. The Juno-106 has played the pads, basses and late-night arpeggios of four decades and shows no sign of going home early.

Youth106 recreates it with one oscillator, one beautiful filter and the chorus that made the whole thing glow.

It's simple enough to learn in an afternoon and good enough to keep for the rest of your life. Press Chorus II. Everybody does.

“Born in 1984. Still out every night.”

  • _ Polyphonic synthesizer

05.Zynabre

ZynAddSubFX ported to Rust. Loads your .xiz and .xmz patches.

Since 2002 the open-source community has made thousands of patches for ZynAddSubFX. Its ADDsynth, SUBsynth and PADsynth engines are unlike anything else.

Zynabre is the same engine, ported module by module to Rust. It opens your original .xiz and .xmz files and plays them the way Zyn does, and that was measured sample for sample.

It has sixteen parts, sixteen kit items and the whole effect rack, all inside a new editor. Abre is Portuguese for “it opens.”

“Twenty years of sounds were waiting behind it.”

  • _ ZynAddSubFX, 2002
  • _ Additive · subtractive · PAD synth
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/zynabre

Vocals

06.BuenoVOX

Natural vocal pitch correction, automatic or MIDI-driven.

Pitch correction usually comes in two flavours: you can hear it, or it doesn't work. BuenoVOX was built for the space between, where the note is right and the singer still sounds like a person.

Let it find the notes automatically, or play the target notes on your MIDI keyboard and it will follow you to the semitone.

Set it tight or set it transparent. You won't hear a robot unless you specifically ask for one.

“Even he sings in tune now.”

  • _ Pitch correction
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/buenovox

07.MuchoVox

A natural vocal harmonizer with pitch correction built in.

Harmonies used to mean hiring singers, or one singer and a very long night. MuchoVox makes the harmonies from the voice you already have.

It's a natural-sounding harmonizer with pitch correction built in. Thirds, fifths and doubles stay human, without chipmunks, without warble and without that tell-tale shimmer.

It's BuenoVOX's bigger sibling, for the moment the chorus needs a whole congregation.

“He came in alone. He left as a choir.”

Effects

08.Mutante

Component-level model of the 1974 Mu-Tron Bi-Phase.

In 1974 a small company in New Jersey put two phasers, two sweep generators and a light bulb inside one box. Then the dub engineers of Kingston got hold of it, and records never sounded quite the same.

Mutante models the Mu-Tron Bi-Phase component by component from the original schematics. That means every coupling capacitor, every diode knee and every photocell, slowly following the light.

The schematics and the bench measurements are published in the repository.

“Your guitar, underwater. Your amplifier, perfectly dry.”

  • _ Mu-Tron Bi-Phase, 1974
  • _ Dual optical phaser
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/mutante

09.th0nk3000

The cult Mac OS 9 granular app, reverse-engineered and back.

In 1996 a little Mac program took any sound you gave it, smashed it into grains and rebuilt it in ways no one could predict. People loved it. Then the operating system it ran on disappeared.

th0nk3000 is a reverse-engineered recreation of Audio Ease's cult Th0nk, back as a modern app and plug-in. This time the source is open, so it can't vanish again.

Drop in a sample and press the button. Each press gives you a different collision, and some of them will end up on your record.

“Nobody knows what happens next. Not even us.”

  • _ Audio Ease Th0nk, 1996
  • _ Granular collider
  • _ Standalone · AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/th0nk3000

Mastering

10.Magnitude

Upward mastering compressor: lifts the quiet, leaves the peaks.

Most compressors make a mix louder by pushing the peaks down. Magnitude works the other way round: it lifts the detail underneath them.

Room tone, reverb tails and the breath before a line all come up into the light, while the peaks stay exactly where you left them. You get density without pumping.

It's a descendant of PRESSURE, the 2006 plug-in by Tom and Jeremy Gersic, reborn in Rust.

“It lifts the quiet. It leaves the loud alone.”

  • _ PRESSURE plug-in, 2006
  • _ Upward compressor
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/magnitude

Mastering

11.Sunbliss

Converter-style mastering clipper and soft saturation.

There's a particular kind of loud that only happens when an analogue-to-digital converter is pushed a little past polite. It's dense and warm, and strangely it never sounds broken.

Sunbliss is a mastering clipper and soft saturator built to behave exactly like that. Every curve came from measuring real audio and reading public manuals, and the method is documented on GitHub.

Turn it up and the record gets louder and warmer, not uglier.

“Loud is a lovely place to spend the summer.”

  • _ Clipper · soft saturation
  • _ AU · VST3 · CLAP
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/sunbliss

Studio tools

12.Live Remote

Control Ableton Live from your phone or tablet's browser.

Every musician who records alone knows the walk: press record, run back to the instrument, catch your breath, miss the downbeat. Then do it all again.

Live Remote puts Ableton Live in your phone or tablet's browser, with Session and Arrangement views, the mixer, transport, and a big red REC button with input meters.

Run it on the computer and open an address on your phone over the same Wi-Fi. There's nothing to install on the phone.

“Press record from the other end of the house.”

  • _ Remote for Ableton Live
  • _ Desktop app
  • _ Open source
> Download github.com/nagualcode/liveremote

Contribute

Every project here is open source, and each one lives in its own GitHub repository with the code, the research notes and the measurements behind it. Contributing doesn't have to mean writing code: playing a plug-in in a real session and telling us what happened is just as valuable. If something crashes, clicks or doesn't sound right, open an issue on that project's page with your system, your DAW and the steps that got you there. If you can fix it, or have an idea for the DSP, the interface or the documentation, fork the repository and send a pull request. Every report and every patch makes these instruments better for everyone who uses them.
